## Abstract

The rapid growth in submissions to machine learning venues has strained the scientific peer-review system and intensified interest in LLM-based automated peer reviewers. However, how good these systems are actually, especially compared to human reviewers at catching scientific gaps, remains poorly understood. In this work, we introduce **PRISM** (**P**eer **R**eview **I**ntelligence via **S**tructured **M**ulti-dimensional assessment), a benchmarking framework that evaluates review quality across four dimensions: **Depth of Analysis**, **Novelty Assessment**, **Flaw Identification & Major Issues Prioritization**, and **Multi-dimensional Constructiveness**. Unlike most existing evaluations based on surface-level metrics like ROUGE and BLEU, or unconstrained LLM-as-a-judge prompting that conflates fluency with rigor, PRISM grounds each dimension in argument mining, retrieval-augmented verification, and consensus-based scoring.

We apply PRISM to benchmark five leading automated reviewer systems and human reviewers on a stratified corpus of 1,000 reviews from ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS. The results reveal that LLMs can match or beat human reviewers on individual dimensions: comparable depth of analysis, stronger novelty verification, and highly accurate critique prioritization. However, no single system consistently matches the balanced performance of the human baseline across all dimensions at once. Each exhibits a distinct specialization profile with characteristic blind spots—failure modes that aggregate metrics miss entirely. The implication is that _LLM reviewers are best understood as targeted supplements to human review, effective within specific dimensions, but unreliable as standalone replacements._

## Method: PRISM as a Structured Review Judge

Scientific peer review is under mounting strain. Submission volumes at major ML venues have grown exponentially—NeurIPS received 21,575 submissions in 2025, while ICML saw a 44.9% year-on-year jump. This growth severely strains the reviewer pool, prompting venues to introduce new policies and raising an important question: **Are LLMs sufficient reviewers to evaluate scientific work?**

PRISM addresses this by evaluating reviews across four independent pipelines, each targeting a different responsibility of scientific peer review. Rather than asking an LLM judge for one holistic score—which risks conflating fluency with rigor—PRISM decomposes the review into evidence units, novelty claims, flaw arguments, and atomic comments. The LLM performs constrained extraction and verification; the final scores are computed analytically.

### What PRISM Measures

| Dimension | Unit of Analysis | Judge Task | Metric Output |
|-----------|------------------|------------|---------------|
| **Depth of Analysis** | Argumentative Discourse Units | Classify claims, premises, topics, and grounding level | Premise Ratio, Grounding Score, DoA |
| **Novelty Assessment** | Verbatim novelty claims | Retrieve prior work and verify literature support | Novelty Score, Support Rate, Strict Support Rate |
| **Flaw Identification** | Distinct flaw arguments | Verify flaws, merge consensus truth, classify severity | Critical Recall, Minor Recall, nCPS |
| **Constructiveness** | Atomic Review Comments | Score helpfulness across five dimensions | Mean Constructiveness Score |

</HighlightedSection>


### Dimension 1: Depth of Analysis (DoA)

A high-quality review must substantiate its judgments with objective, well-grounded premises—not rely on generic assertions.

**How it works:** We extract core review sections (Summary, Strengths, Weaknesses) and break them into **Argumentative Discourse Units (ADUs)**. Each ADU is classified by:
- **Argumentative role:** *Claim* (conclusion) or *Premise* (supporting evidence)
- **Aspect topic:** Novelty, Methodology, Experiments, or Clarity

Premises are assessed for **grounding level** $g(p) \in \{0,1,2\}$:
- **Level 0:** Vague/Generic (e.g., "the method seems limited")
- **Level 1:** Internal (references the manuscript directly)  
- **Level 2:** External (references broader scientific literature)

The DoA score combines **Premise Ratio** (evidence coverage) and **Average Grounding Score** (evidence quality) via harmonic mean—penalizing imbalance between quantity and rigor.

### Dimension 2: Novelty Assessment

Genuine novelty judgment requires situating a paper's contributions within prior literature. Our pipeline verifies whether a reviewer's novelty claims are supported or refuted by retrievable prior work.

**Three-stage pipeline:**

1. **Extraction:** A constrained LLM extracts the paper's core task, contribution anchors, and verbatim novelty claims $\mathcal{C} = \{c_1,\ldots,c_n\}$
2. **Retrieval:** Deterministic Semantic Scholar queries retrieve prior publications, filtered and diversified via Maximal Marginal Relevance to form candidate pool $\mathcal{B}$
3. **Verification:** For each claim-candidate pair $(c_i, b_j)$, an LLM judge returns a discrete evidence-support score $s(c_i, b_j) \in \{-2,-1,0,+1,+2\}$

### Dimension 3: Flaw Identification & Prioritization

Effective peer review requires both accurate diagnosis of errors and clear structural organization. Since the absolute number of flaws in any manuscript is unobservable, we establish a relative "ground truth" using a **consensus mechanism** that merges findings from both human and LLM reviewers.

**Pipeline stages:**
1. **Extraction:** Isolate critical sections (Summary, Weaknesses, Questions) from human and LLM reviews; parse concurrently to extract distinct flaw arguments
2. **Consensus Verification:** An LLM judge evaluates all extracted flaws against paper context, discarding invalid or hallucinated critiques; verified findings are merged into consensus ground truth
3. **Classification:** Flaws are classified as *Critical* (methodological errors, flawed proofs) or *Minor* (typos, formatting)
4. **Positional Recovery:** Valid flaws are mapped back to their sequential position in the review text

**Scoring metrics:**
- **Diagnostic Coverage:** Severity-stratified recall (Critical/Minor Recall)
- **Structural Ranking:** Normalized Critique Prioritization Score ($nCPS$), inspired by NDCG, measuring whether critical flaws appear before minor ones

### Dimension 4: Multi-dimensional Constructiveness

A review's real value lies in its ability to help authors improve. We decompose constructiveness into five dimensions, each rated 0-2 per Atomic Review Comment (ARC):

| Dimension | What It Measures | Example |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|
| **Actionability** ($D_1$) | Clear, implementable guidance vs. vague opinions | "Add ablation study on component X" vs. "Needs more experiments" |
| **Specificity** ($D_2$) | Pinpoints concrete elements (sections, equations) | "Equation 3 assumes independence" vs. "The math is wrong" |
| **Justification** ($D_3$) | Assertions backed by reasoning or evidence | "Batch size affects convergence because..." vs. "This won't work" |
| **Solution** ($D_4$) | Proposes improvement path vs. just highlighting problems | "Consider using method Y from [ref]" vs. "Novelty is limited" |
| **Tone** ($D_5$) | Professional and encouraging language | Neutral vs. hostile/dismissive |

The **Mean Constructiveness Score (MCS)** averages across all ARCs: $MCS(R) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} CLC(c_j)$

## Qualitative Assessment

PRISM is designed to make judge behavior inspectable. Instead of only reporting a final scalar, each pipeline exposes the intermediate units that led to the score: which text span became a claim, which span became evidence, which flaw entered the consensus set, and which comment was considered actionable.

| System | DoA Score | Premise Ratio | Grounding Score |
|--------|:---------:|:-------------:|:---------------:|
| **Human** | **0.494** | 0.567 | 0.475 |
| CycleReviewer | 0.484 | 0.614 | 0.431 |
| DeepReview | 0.483 | 0.596 | 0.441 |
| Reviewer2 | 0.468 | 0.558 | 0.436 |
| SEA | 0.459 | 0.549 | 0.435 |
| TreeReview | 0.409 | 0.520 | 0.394 |

**Key Insight:** Human reviewers achieve high DoA through both strong premise ratio AND cognitive alignment that prioritizes methodology. The best LLMs (DeepReview, CycleReviewer) close the gap by generating highly robust premises, using structural completeness to compensate for slight gaps in empirical grounding.

## Key Takeaways

<HighlightedSection>

### No Single System Wins

Each LLM reviewer excels in a distinct niche but exhibits characteristic blind spots:

| System | Strength | Weakness |
|--------|----------|----------|
| **Reviewer2** | Exhaustive flaw scanning (highest recall) | Low solution provision |
| **DeepReview** | Constructive feedback (actionable, professional) | Slightly lower flaw recall |
| **SEA** | Novelty verification (highest literature support) | Lower constructiveness |
| **CycleReviewer** | Strong analytical depth | High hallucination rate |
| **TreeReview** | — | Surface-level trap (24% effort on formatting) |

</HighlightedSection>

### Deployment Recommendations

Since no single system dominates all four dimensions, we recommend **targeted ensemble deployment**:

- **Use Reviewer2** for exhaustive flaw scanning — it catches critical issues human reviewers miss
- **Use DeepReview** for constructive feedback drafting — it provides actionable, solution-oriented suggestions
- **Use SEA** for novelty-grounding checks — it verifies claims against prior literature effectively
- **Use human reviewers** for final judgment — they provide the most balanced, cognitively aligned assessment

These systems are most effective as **specialist co-pilots within a human-assisted pipeline** rather than autonomous reviewers.
